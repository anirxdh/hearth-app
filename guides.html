Six emergencies, four pictures each. Simple enough for a ten-year-old, calm enough for the worst hour. Vetted steps, printed big, working offline. No AI writes these words.

If someone falls

Print cardSend to family▶ 2-min video
For Robert: he takes a blood thinner, so a bumped head always needs a doctor, even if he says he feels fine. His ER sheet is one tap away.
1
Stay with them. Talk softly.Do not pull them up. Take one slow breath together.
2
Look before you move.Head, hips, wrists. Any blood? Ask: "Where does it hurt?"
3
Hurt, dizzy, or not sure? Call 911.Do not help them stand. Keep them warm and still.
4
Feeling fine? Up slowly, with a chair.Roll to the side, kneel, hands on the seat. Rest after.

If someone is choking

Print card▶ 2-min video
1
Ask: "Can you talk?"If they can cough or speak, let them cough. If they cannot, act now.
2
Lean them forward. 5 back blows.Hit firmly between the shoulder blades with your palm heel.
3
5 tummy pushes.Fist just above the belly button. Pull in and up, hard.
4
Repeat: 5 and 5. Call 911.Keep going until it comes out or help arrives. Shout for anyone nearby.

If you think it's a stroke

Print card▶ 2-min video
Remember one word: F.A.S.T. Face, Arms, Speech, Time. Any one sign means call 911 now, even if it goes away.
1
Face: ask for a big smile.Does one side droop or look uneven?
2
Arms: raise both, eyes closed.Does one arm drift down on its own?
3
Speech: "The sky is blue today."Is it slurred, jumbled, or missing?
4
Time: call 911 this minute.Say the word "stroke." Write down when it started.

If there's bad bleeding

Print card▶ 2-min video
1
Press hard with a clean cloth.Both hands if you need. Hard pressing is what stops it.
2
Keep pressing. Do not peek.Lifting the cloth restarts the bleeding. Count 10 slow minutes.
3
Soaking through? Add more on top.Never remove layers. This is a 911 call.
4
Raise it. Keep them warm.Injured part up on a pillow. A blanket over them. Stay close.

If someone has a seizure

Print card▶ 2-min video
1
Make space. Soft under the head.Push chairs and tables away. A folded jacket works.
2
Do not hold them down.Nothing in the mouth. Not fingers, not water, not pills.
3
Look at a clock. Time it.Longer than 5 minutes, or a second one starts: call 911.
4
After: roll them on their side.Stay until they are fully awake. Speak gently. It's scary to wake up lost.

If there's chest pain

Print card▶ 2-min video
For Robert: his heart history and medication list are on his ER sheet. Hand it to the paramedics. Open Robert's ER sheet
1
Call 911 right away.Do not wait to "see if it passes." Do not drive them yourself.
2
Sit them down. Knees up.Loosen the collar. Slow breaths together: in for 4, out for 4.
3
Aspirin? Only if it's safe.Not allergic, and their doctor never said no: chew one adult aspirin.
4
Stay. Unlock the door. ER sheet out.Meds, allergies, contacts: one page, ready to hand over.
